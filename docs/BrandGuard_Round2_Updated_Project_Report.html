1. Understanding of the problem statement

Organisations now publish through many hands at once: employees, agencies, freelancers and AI writing tools. Each contributor interprets the brand slightly differently, and nobody sees the whole picture before content goes live. The result is content with wrong or outdated facts, unsupported promotional claims, off-tone language, misused terminology, off-palette visuals and inaccessible design. These errors usually become visible only after publication, when the damage to credibility is already done.

The brief asks for more than a spell-checker or a content filter. A useful solution must:

  • review content before it goes live, against approved guidelines, facts and assets;
  • detect issues in tone, terminology, messaging, visual consistency, accessibility and unsupported claims;
  • explain every issue, cite the guideline or source behind it, and suggest a correction that keeps the creator's purpose instead of simply rejecting the work;
  • support collaboration through comments, approvals, revision history and role-based review;
  • reveal recurring mistakes and unclear guidelines over time;
  • balance automation with human control, while protecting confidential material and being transparent about AI use.

Root causes & the gap we address

  • No shared source of truth: Guidelines sit in scattered PDFs, emails and people's heads.
  • Outdated information: Old specifications, prices and logos keep circulating, especially with external agencies.
  • AI tools that do not know the company: Generic LLMs write fluent text with invented claims because they cannot verify what the company has approved.
  • Manual review does not scale: A few brand managers cannot inspect every post, so checks become slow, inconsistent or skipped.
  • No feedback loop: Mistakes are fixed one by one; nobody asks whether the guideline behind a repeated mistake is itself unclear.
Our reading of the core challenge is trust. Reviewers will rely on an automated system only if each finding is grounded in an approved source, can be explained in plain language, and can always be overruled by a person.

3. Proposed solution & Round 2 Build Status

BrandGuard is a pre-publication review layer. A creator submits content; BrandGuard checks it against the organisation's brand knowledge base and returns findings, each with an explanation, the rule that triggered it and a suggested fix. People stay in control: nothing is changed automatically, risky decisions need a reviewer, and every action is recorded.

Where we stand in Round 2 (The Build): The complete 9-step central workflow is live and running end to end. A judge opening our public URL in an incognito window lands directly in the pre-seeded workspace with 3 live submissions (including the exact BG-001 SmartFlow launch post from Round 1), a 1-click Role Switcher (Creator, Reviewer, Manager, Admin), an Apple-inspired HIG Studio UI, and a 27-step interactive guided tour accessible from the floating ? helper button.

Six design principles (all active in Round 2)

  • Evidence-grounded: No finding is shown without citing a rule ID, source document, owner, version and exact verified source quote checked by the Evidence Validator (backend/engine.py).
  • Hybrid engine: Deterministic code handles exact checks (banned terms, expired facts, WCAG 2.1 contrast, alt text, CTA links, palette distance). The AI layer (Claude API with offline heuristic fallback) handles nuanced tone and unsupported claim judgment.
  • Intent-preserving suggestions: Each suggested rewrite makes the minimal change required and explicitly states what creator intent was preserved.
  • Human control: Creators accept, custom-edit or dismiss with a mandatory written reason. High-severity findings dismissed by a creator automatically escalate to a reviewer.
  • Confidentiality and transparency: Text inside [[double brackets]] is redacted locally before any external AI call, and every run is logged with a SHA-256 payload hash in the Audit & AI Trust Log.
  • Learning loop: High dismissal rates (≥ 40%) automatically flag ambiguous guidelines in the Insights Dashboard with a 1-click rule clarification flow.

7. What changed from our original Round 1 proposal, and why

  1. Added a 1-Click Judge Role-Switcher & Pre-Seeded Incognito Workspace (alongside formal login): In Round 1 we proposed standard token login. To ensure hackathon judges opening our live link in an incognito window can immediately test all 4 personas (Creator, Reviewer, Manager, Admin) without typing credentials, we added a 1-click Role Switcher in the sidebar that dynamically issues real HMAC-SHA256 tokens for each persona while keeping the formal PBKDF2 login modal available.
  2. Added an Interactive 27-Step Guided Tour & Role Guide: To maximize comprehensibility for first-time visitors, we built a custom WCAG-compliant spotlight tour engine (tour.js) with choreographed workspace transitions and a floating ? helper button.
  3. Client-Side HTML5 Canvas Dominant Color Extraction: Instead of requiring heavy server-side image binaries for Round 2 palette checks, we implemented client-side HTML5 Canvas pixel sampling paired with backend perceptual RGB distance math ($\Delta E$), delivering instant visual compliance checks in the browser.

8. Development plan & Three Major Improvements for Round 3

Round 3 Improvement What It Is & What It Changes for the User
Improvement 1: Multi-Format Document Ingestion & Layout-Aware Parsing (PDF & DOCX) What it is: A server-side document ingestion pipeline using pypdf, python-docx, and Pillow that parses uploaded PDF brochures and Word drafts into structured sections, headings, tables, embedded images, and hyperlinks.
What it changes for the user: Users can drag-and-drop real marketing PDFs and DOCX files instead of pasting plain text, and every finding is anchored to its exact page number, paragraph index, or embedded asset.
Improvement 2: Semantic Fact & Claim Retrieval via Vector Embeddings What it is: A semantic retrieval layer over the Brand Knowledge Base that embeds approved facts and claims into vector space and computes cosine similarity against candidate sentences before invoking the AI judge.
What it changes for the user: Catches paraphrased or indirectly worded factual contradictions (e.g., "converses across nearly two dozen global tongues" vs. F-12: Supports 5 languages) that keyword or regex patterns cannot match, while displaying a semantic similarity score on each finding.
Improvement 3: Multimodal Vision-Model Brand Asset & Logo Verification What it is: A multimodal vision-inspection pipeline for uploaded campaign graphics and banners that checks logo clear-space ratios, detects stretched or unapproved logo variants, and measures text-over-image contrast across complex photographic backgrounds.
What it changes for the user: Design and social-media teams get automated visual governance on complete ad creatives—catching logo misuse and illegible text overlays before campaigns go live.
